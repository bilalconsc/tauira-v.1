This encounter can be processed through familiar reflective theories as an instance of sensitive facilitation, reflection-in-action, or later professional learning about care. Those readings remain useful. But Tauira should hold the event as more than a lesson in better handling difficult topics.

Rhizomatically, this encounter links concept to affect, affect to temporality, and temporality to role instability. It therefore extends beyond the individual student response and becomes part of a wider map of how knowledge enters bodies, memories, and ethical relations. In this network, sadness is not outside learning. It is one of the ways the pedagogical assemblage makes learning happen. See [[enc-2025-T1-W3-L2]] for the recurrence of affective shifts in the room, and [[memo-003]] for the threshold map connecting concept, identity, and pedagogical tempo.
