This encounter opened with low affective energy. The assessment rubric, distributed the previous week, seemed to weigh on the room. Boredom was visible in the first fifteen minutes — bodies angled away from the board, minimal eye contact with the task.

The turn came when S3, seated at the cluster of round tables near the window, held up their laminated rubric and remarked, deadpan: *"This looks like something you'd find at a funeral."* The laughter that followed was collective and immediate. See [[enc-2025-T1-W5-L3]] for the recurrence of this affective pattern.

The assemblage of round tables, laminated deadline, and S3's comedic interruption formed what I am calling the *table-deadline-joke cluster* — a repeating configuration across T1 that shaped the affective texture of writing lessons. For analytic reflection see [[memo-001]].
