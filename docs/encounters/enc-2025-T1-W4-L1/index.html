W4-L1 — What If Knowledge Had No Centre?

March 17, 2025

Research Question Alignment

RQ1
A lesson shaped as much by what was absent as by what was present — three students missing, low ambient energy, the whiteboard question suspended in the room.
RQ1a
The absent students structured my experience throughout the lesson, creating a persistent undertow of concern that coloured every exchange.
RQ1b
The rubric-table-theory triangle emerged as a recurring assemblage: assessment constraint, physical arrangement, and Deleuzian framing in productive tension.

Fieldnote

Scene

Room 14, tables rearranged into two offset rows. Three students absent. The whiteboard reads: 'What if knowledge had no centre?' Low ambient energy — the room feels thinner without those three bodies.

Agency

I introduced rhizomatic thinking through a map analogy: what if knowledge grew sideways, not upward? I moved table-by-table asking each group to name their entry point into the week's work. The rearranged tables supported this — no obvious front.

Care

The absent students weighed on me throughout. Without the live experience of this session, the theoretical introduction would be irretrievable for them. I wrote their names in my notebook at the end.

Vignette

The whiteboard question hung in the room like a kite whose string I had let go.

Participants

Round Tables (Material) Assessment Rubric (Knowledge) Rhizomatic Thinking (Knowledge) Boredom (Instinctual)

Assemblages

  • rubric-table-theory triangle

Three students were absent. Their absence structured this lesson as much as those present — the room felt different, thinner. The [[Assessment Rubric]] from W3 was still on several desks, a material residue of the previous week’s constraint.

The introduction of [[Rhizomatic Thinking]] through Deleuze and Guattari produced a slow, cautious engagement. I moved table-by-table, asking each group their entry point. The rearranged tables — less clearly oriented toward any centre — afforded this differently from the standard arc configuration.

This was the first lesson I explicitly theorised the table arrangement. See [[memo-002]] for gap analysis and [[essay-001]] for the theoretical synthesis across all three encounters.
